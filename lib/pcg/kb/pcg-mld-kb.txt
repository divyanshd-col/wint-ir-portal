What are MLDs and how do they work
Q: What are Market Linked Debentures (MLDs)?
A: An MLD (Market Linked Debenture) is a debt security whose return is linked to the performance of an underlying market instrument, such as an index. Unlike a conventional bond, which pays a fixed or predetermined coupon, your return on an MLD depends on its payoff structure and the performance of the underlying, both of which are disclosed on the platform.

Q: What do you mean by returns being linked to the market?
A: Your MLD's return is derived from the performance of an underlying market instrument — an index such as the NIFTY 50 or NIFTY Midcap 150. The specific index and structure are disclosed on the platform, along with your MLD's other terms and conditions. Depending on that structure, a rise or fall in the underlying index can change your final payoff.

Q: How will my returns be calculated?
A: Your MLD has predefined observation dates and a predefined method for determining the entry and exit levels, as disclosed on the platform. The difference between the entry and exit levels, combined with the payoff structure disclosed on the platform, determines your return.

Q: If I buy an MLD three months after its issue or listing, will the entry and exit levels remain the same?
A: The predefined entry and exit observation levels used to calculate your returns are fixed and do not change based on when you purchase it. The price at which you purchase the MLD in the secondary market can be different from its original issue price, and this can change your effective return.

Returns, protection, and taxation
Q: Will I receive monthly interest on an MLD?
A: MLDs do not pay regular monthly interest like a conventional coupon-paying bond. Your return is determined by the payoff structure disclosed on the platform and is paid at maturity or on the payout date(s) specified there.

Q: Is my capital protected in an MLD?
A: Capital protection depends on the specific MLD you invest in. Check the Key Information Document (KID) disclosed on the platform — it states whether your MLD offers principal (capital) protection or downside protection, and the conditions that apply. Even when an MLD is principal-protected, repayment still depends on the issuer meeting its obligations.

Q: Can I consider an MLD risk-free?
A: No. MLDs are not risk-free. Key risks include issuer or credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, and structure or model risk. Even a principal-protected MLD carries issuer or credit risk — the protection is subject to the issuer's ability to meet its repayment obligations.

Q: How are MLDs taxed?
A: MLDs have a specific tax treatment under Section 50AA of the Income-tax Act. For MLDs covered under this provision, your gains on transfer or on redemption or maturity are treated as short-term capital gains, irrespective of your holding period, and taxed at the rate applicable to you. Tax treatment depends on your individual situation, so check with your tax advisor before you invest.
