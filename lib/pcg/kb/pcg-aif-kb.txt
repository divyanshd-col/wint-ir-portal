Risks
Q: What are the key risks associated with the fund?
A: There are two key risks you should know about. Liquidity Risk: The fund has a 3-year tenure and liquidity is offered to you on a best-effort basis, so your capital may not be available on demand before maturity. Credit/Default Risk: Your returns depend on the underlying issuers meeting their repayment obligations, and debt investments carry credit risk. The AIF follows a rigorous underwriting and credit assessment process and is designed to diversify the portfolio across approximately 30-40 bonds. At the fund's full capacity of Rs. 100 crore, each bond makes up approximately 3% of the overall portfolio, which limits the impact of any single issuer default on the fund. The income generated across the diversified portfolio also gives you a cushion against losses from an individual credit event. If multiple bonds default at the same time, this can have some impact on your principal — however, given the diversification, a significant loss of principal is unlikely.

Taxation
Q: What is the taxation for an NRI investing in a Category II AIF?
A: Your tax treatment as an NRI is primarily determined by the nature of income generated by the AIF, rather than simply by whether you invested through an NRE or NRO account. For Category I and Category II AIFs, the tax framework provides a pass-through mechanism — income generated by the AIF is taxed in your hands as though you had made the investment directly. The Income Tax Department has specifically explained this principle for Category I and Category II AIFs. If you do not submit the required documents such as TRC, Form 41/10F and No PE Declaration, TDS will be deducted at the maximum marginal rate. If you submit the required documents, TDS will be withheld at the lower applicable rate under the relevant DTAA. For a debt-focused Category II AIF, the flow is — the AIF earns income → income is allocated or distributed to you → you are taxed in India on the applicable taxable income. The tax rate depends on the character of that income. For example, interest or income from debt investments is taxable in India at the applicable rate for you, depending on the nature of the income and applicable tax provisions. Capital gains are taxed according to the applicable capital-gains provisions and the nature or holding period of the underlying asset or AIF unit. Business income has specific pass-through rules and is taxed at the fund level rather than being passed through in the same manner as other income. If you are a tax resident of another country, the relevant Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) can provide a more beneficial tax treatment, subject to the applicable conditions and documentation such as a Tax Residency Certificate. The Income Tax Department confirms that DTAA provisions apply where they are more beneficial to you as a non-resident.

AIF Basics (General Glossary)
Q: What is an AIF (Alternative Investment Fund)?
A: An AIF is a privately pooled investment fund that invests in assets other than traditional investments like stocks and bonds. It is designed for sophisticated investors such as HNIs and institutions who have a higher risk appetite and want more advanced investment opportunities.

Q: What are the types of Alternative Investment Funds?
A: AIFs are classified based on the type of assets they invest in and their investment strategy — each type serves a different purpose and risk profile. Venture Capital Fund (VCF): invests in early-stage startups and new businesses with high growth potential, often in technology or innovative sectors. Angel Fund: a sub-category of VCF where funds are raised from angel investors to invest in very early-stage startups. Private Equity (PE) Fund: invests in established companies through equity or equity-linked instruments to drive growth and value creation. Special Situation Fund: invests in stressed or distressed assets and may participate in resolution under insolvency frameworks. Debt Fund: invests in debt instruments of companies, listed or unlisted, aiming for steady income with relatively lower risk than equity. Infrastructure Fund: focuses on long-term infrastructure projects like roads, power, or telecom. SME Fund: invests in small and medium enterprises, supporting their growth and expansion. Hedge Fund: uses complex strategies including derivatives, leverage, and trading in various markets to generate high returns. Social Venture Fund: invests in businesses with a social impact objective, where returns may be secondary to societal benefits.

Category II AIFs include funds like private equity and debt funds that do not take leverage, except for operational needs, and invest in a structured manner. Category III AIFs use complex trading strategies and may use leverage, including investments in listed or unlisted derivatives, aiming for higher returns.
